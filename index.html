The paper we're extending

What Lopez-Lira & Tang found

They fed 134,129 headlines (Oct 2021 to Dec 2023, 4,106 U.S. firms) to GPT-4 with one simple prompt, then traded on the answers. Honestly, the results surprised me.

38 bps

Average daily return going long good news and short bad news, before costs.

3.28

Annualized Sharpe ratio. The CAPM and five-factor alphas are basically the same size.

3×

The short side earned about three times the long side. Bad news sinks in slowly.

20 bps

The round-trip trading cost where the whole strategy stops making money.

Bigger models, better predictions

Sharpe ratio by language model. The small ones actually lose money, and that "threshold" is exactly what the paper's theory predicts.

Source: Lopez-Lira & Tang (2024), Table 4.

But the edge is fading

Our contribution

What we're adding

We didn't want to just re-run the paper. Each extension below answers a question the authors left open, and each one ties back to their own theory.

A fresh test, after publication

We score 2026 headlines the paper never saw, using models trained before that news existed. If the market learned from the paper, the alpha should be smaller now.

tests Theorem 2: market efficiency

A clean model-size ladder

The paper compares models from different companies. We use one family, Gemma 3, at 1B, 4B and 12B parameters, so the only thing that changes is size.

tests Theorem 1: the size threshold

Signal decay and real costs

How fast does the signal die, and can you actually trade it? We measure the information coefficient at each horizon and solve for the break-even trading cost.

turns a backtest into a trading question

Risk, or real information?

We check whether the alpha survives FF3, FF5 and q-factors plus momentum and reversal, split the sample at ChatGPT's launch, and test whether the score predicts the next earnings surprise.

every robustness check in our guideline

The exact prompt

We're using the paper's prompt word for word. So, if our results differ, it's because of the data or the model, not because we asked differently.

Forget all your previous instructions. Pretend you are a financial expert. You are a financial expert with stock recommendation experience. Answer "YES" if good news, "NO" if bad news, or "UNKNOWN" if uncertain in the first line. Then elaborate with one short and concise sentence on the next line. Is this headline good or bad for the stock price of {company_name} in the short term?
Headline: {headline}

YES = +1, UNKNOWN = 0, NO = −1. Temperature 0, so every run gives the same answer.
